01 · Solution Summary

One Q-SYS platform for the whole Breezeway

Mason Technologies proposes an all-Q-SYS audio and video system for the Building 2 Breezeway: a line array stage system and an LED video wall at each end, 14 individually controlled loudspeakers along the Breezeway, eight wireless microphone channels, and touch panel and iPad control with one-touch event presets.

Two stage systems

Each end of the Breezeway gets left and right line arrays of four Q-SYS PL-LA8, plus two PL-SUB12 subwoofers flown behind them — speech, presentations, DJs and small live bands at about a 70 ft throw.

Fourteen independent zones

Seven locations, one in each gap between columns, each with a street-facing AD-S12 and an interior-facing AD-S8T. Every loudspeaker has its own amplifier channel for delay, level, EQ, mute and routing.

Three operating modes

Stage Performance, BGM / Paging and Street Event. Selecting a mode recalls the right sources, zones and delay times, so the operator never calculates timing.

Eight wireless channels

Handheld, bodypack and headset options on every channel, with five remote antennas across the Breezeway and café. A portable console and Dante stage box move to wherever the event is.

Two outdoor LED walls

A 1.2 mm BlueSquareX Xterra wall at each stage. Any of five sources — rack PC, signage, Apple TV or a presenter laptop — can play on either wall, or both.

Built for outdoors, ready to grow

All loudspeakers are IP54 rated. Three amplifier channels stay spare, and the system is designed to extend to Building 1 over the campus AV network.

Every distributed loudspeaker is its own zone

All 14 Breezeway loudspeakers have a dedicated amplifier and DSP channel. Street and interior speakers are never paired, and neighboring columns are never combined. That is what lets Q-SYS set a different delay on each loudspeaker when a stage moves, since each one sits at a different distance from the sound source.

Day to day, the iPad shows only a few simple zones. The 14 discrete channels stay available underneath for tuning and event presets.

The satellite rack amplifiers turn the three longest speaker runs into short ones. No worst-case run loses more than about 1.1 dB on 12 AWG cable. The amplifiers plug into dedicated circuits from MDC's electrical contractor: five at the main rack and two at the satellite rack.

All video travels as AV over IP inside Q-SYS, on the same switches as the audio. Five sources can feed two LED walls in any combination — both walls can show the same source or different ones. Try it on the Controls tab.

Quick setup

No console needed for a podium mic or DJ: a Dante XLR box plugs into any Dante port, takes power from it, and gives four XLR inputs straight into Q-SYS.

Eight channels, three mic styles each

  • Two Shure SLXD4QDAN+ four-channel receivers, cascaded to share one antenna feed, with analog outputs into the Core and Dante to the portable console
  • A handheld (Beta 58A), a bodypack and a Countryman H6 headset for each channel; only one transmitter is live on a channel at a time
  • Rechargeable SB903 batteries for every transmitter, with two 8-bay chargers
  • Final antenna positions, gain settings and frequency coordination come from Mason's RF design and scan

Two antenna sides, no dropouts

Five Shure UA864US antennas: left, middle and right of the Breezeway, and two in the café. Each receiver has an A and a B input and always uses the stronger one. A mic anywhere in the Breezeway or café is seen by both sides. Turn on the antenna layer on the site plan to see them.

AV network · three VLANs on two NETGEAR M4250 switches

Q-LAN

Cores, amplifiers, NV-21-HU endpoints, touch panel, Bluetooth plates and control interfaces. Gigabit, QoS with clock traffic at top priority, IGMP snooping and EEE off — set by the switch's built-in Q-SYS profile.

Dante

Console, stage box, Dante XLR boxes and wireless receivers on their own VLAN with the switch's Dante profile. The Core joins both Q-LAN and Dante.

Control

WattBox and UPS units, music player, signage player, rack PC and Apple TV. One uplink to MDC's network gives these internet access; Q-LAN and Dante stay on the AV switches.

Each switch: 40 PoE++ ports, 8 SFP+ fiber ports and a 2,880 W PoE budget. Ports used: 38 of 40 in the main rack, 21 in the satellite rack. Worst-case PoE load is under 600 W and under 400 W. Both racks have a lithium-ion online UPS (3000VA main, 2000VA satellite) carrying processing, network, wireless and video — not the amplifiers.
